# My Recommended Resources for the Curious Engineer

IT is continuously evolving, so in my mindset I need to stay up to date with the most recent trends and technologies. 
This is part of my job requirement, but also part of my character: being curious about what is to come. 
I have built up a list of resources I keep coming back to and refer to.

It is a list: the books, channels and feeds I genuinely follow and some recommend.
It is opinionated and aimed at experienced engineers, but most of it works for any level.
I try to keep this current, and I will revisit it as things change.
Like I used to follow a lot on Twitter/X but the environment became too toxic and I shifted away from it.

A small note on language: most of this is in English, but I have mixed in a few Dutch-language sources.

---

## Books

My goal is to read 2 technical books a year.

### Mindset and craft

- **The Phoenix Project** - Gene Kim, Kevin Behr, George Spafford. A novel about an IT-manager rescuing a failing project. This was so recognisable and actually a fun read, because I could map some characters in the book onto colleges.
- **The Pragmatic Programmer (20th Anniversary Edition)** - David Thomas, Andrew Hunt. Still the best single book on how to actually work as a developer. I wrote a [short review]({{ site.baseurl }}/the-pragmatic-programmer/) of it earlier.
- **Clean Code** and **Clean Architecture** - Robert C. Martin. Great starter book. A lot of it seems common sense, but Uncle Bob took the time to write it down. Read them critically.
- **Building a Second Brain** - Tiago Forte. Not strictly engineering, but it changed how I capture and reuse knowledge. I wrote about [my own setup]({{ site.baseurl }}/building-a-second-brain/).
- **Scrum: The Art of Doing Twice the Work in Half the Time** - Jeff Sutherland. Must-read if you want to start with agile development.
- **The Software Craftsman** - Sandro Mancuso. A guide on how to be a capable IT professional.

### Architecture and design

- **Domain-Driven Design** - Eric Evans. The "blue book". This is the bible for designing software systems.
- **Implementing Domain-Driven Design** - Vaughn Vernon. The "red book". More detailed and harder, but tons of useful information and patterns.
- **Release It!** - Michael Nygard. Stability and resilience patterns for production systems. Loved it. I reviewed it [here]({{ site.baseurl }}/release-it/).
- **Effective Java** - Joshua Block. Must-read for a Java developer.

## YouTube channels

YouTube is where I keep a pulse on the industry. Not every channel deserves the same attention, so I have added a watch frequency to each one.

The scale:
- 💫 high signal, low time cost. I rarely skip an upload.
- 🌟 consistently worth your time. I watch most of it.
- ⭐ useful sometimes, but a lot of it will not be relevant to you.

### Development and engineering

- **[Fireship](https://www.youtube.com/@Fireship)** 💫 Short, fast, genuinely informative. The "100 seconds" and news videos are the most efficient way to stay aware of new tech. News, JavaScript and AI focus.
- **[Devoxx](https://www.youtube.com/@DevoxxForever)** 💫 Free, high-quality conference talks from Devoxx. Java and other JVM-languages focussed.
- **[ThePrimeagen](https://www.youtube.com/@ThePrimeTimeagen)** 🌟 Opinionated takes on engineering, performance and developer culture. A lot about AI and japping. Entertaining.
- **[GOTO Conferences](https://www.youtube.com/@GOTO-)** ⭐ Free, high-quality conference talks from serious practitioners.
- **[Chris Titus Tech](https://www.youtube.com/@ChrisTitusTech)** ⭐ Interesting mix of Windows, Linux and Mac japping. 

### Security

- **[John Hammond](https://www.youtube.com/@_JohnHammond)** 🌟 Malware analysis, CTFs and practical security walkthroughs. I ignore the Minecraft stuff.
- **[NahamSec](https://www.youtube.com/@NahamSec)** ⭐ Bug bounty, CTF,.. A lot of clickbait, but somethimes interesting.

### Others

- **[TechLead](https://www.youtube.com/@TechLead)** ⭐ Watchout: as ex-Google, ex-Facebook, he is a scammer and sad person in general, but he has some interesting takes on technology. 
- **[Marques Brownlee](https://www.youtube.com/@mkbhd)** ⭐ Gadgets master. Fun to watch and learn about the general tech consumer market. 
- **[Linus Tech Tips](https://www.youtube.com/@LinusTechTips)** ⭐ Hardware master. A lot about gaming, which doesn't interest me. 
- **[Lex Fridman](https://www.youtube.com/@lexfridman)** ⭐ Interesting deep interviews, but unfortunately it became more political.

## Newsletters, blogs and feeds

This is where I do most of my day-to-day reading.

- **Hacker News** ([news.ycombinator.com](https://news.ycombinator.com)) ⭐ The front page is still the best single signal for what the engineering world is discussing. Read it for the comments as much as the links.
- **Tweakers** ([tweakers.net](https://tweakers.net)) 💫 Daily newsletter: Dutch-language with tech news and reviews, best in his kind in Benelux.
- **Data News** ([datanews.knack.be](https://datanews.knack.be)) ⭐ Belgian IT and business-technology news. Useful for staying aware of what is happening in our local market and sector.
- **Kordon app** ([newsletter](https://kordon.app/cybersecurity-news/)) 🌟 weekly newsletter that is very interesting. I don't use the products, buy I only consume the newsletter.

## How I actually use this

A list like this is only useful if it does not overwhelm you. My approach:

- **Books are for depth.** I read slowly and take notes — see my [second brain post]({{ site.baseurl }}/building-a-second-brain/). One good book a month beats ten skimmed.
- **YouTube is for breadth and energy.** I keep the "watch all" channels in my subscriptions and let the rest surface by topic.
- **Newsletters and feeds are for the pulse.** A daily skim of Hacker News and a couple of newsletters is enough to never be blindsided.
- **Podcasts are for the in-between time** that would otherwise be wasted.

The goal is not to consume everything. It is to build a small, trusted set of sources and then spend the saved time actually building things.
